## Why Alan's v1 leaves money on the table

Alan's original script is **friendly** — but it's a *pitch-shaped pitch*. Three problems:

1. **It tells, doesn't ask.** From line 3 it's already explaining what DASH does. The prospect's brain throws up the sales wall.
2. **No problem awareness.** It never makes the merchant *feel* the cost of staying with Groupon. No pain = no urgency = no close.
3. **No consequence questions.** It explains the offer (cheap, no contract, 90 days free) — but never makes the merchant say out loud, "I need to fix this."

NEPQ flips it: **the prospect does the persuading.** You ask. They feel. They close themselves.

---

## TONALITY RULES (Read this before reading the script)

These matter more than the words:

1. **Slight downward inflection at the end of every question.** Confident, not pleading.
2. **Slow down.** Pause after every prospect answer for 1–2 seconds before responding. Silence is leverage.
3. **Sound mildly skeptical, not enthusiastic.** Miner's biggest insight: enthusiastic sellers feel like used-car salesmen. Detached curiosity feels like a doctor.
4. **Use their words back to them.** If they say "yeah it's been slow lately" — your next sentence starts with "So when you say slow..."
5. **Never argue. Never pitch. Never close hard.** When they object, you don't defend — you ask another question.
6. **The smile only comes at the end.** Not at the open. The open is calm, professional, almost bored.

## OUTBOUND COLD CALL — NEPQ v2

### STAGE 1 — CONNECTING (the pattern interrupt)

**YOU**
*(calm, slightly bored tonality — NOT enthusiastic)*
> "Hey — this is [Name] from DASH. I know I'm calling you out of the blue, so I'll be quick… is this [Owner First Name]?"

**THEM**
> "Yeah, who's this?"

**YOU**
> "[Name] with DASH. Real quick — you guys are listed on Groupon, right? I just want to make sure I'm calling the right kind of business before I waste your time."

> ☝️ Why this works: you set the frame that you might *not* sell to them. That breaks every Groupon-merchant defensive script they've ever heard. You sound like a screener, not a seller.

---

### STAGE 2 — SITUATION (gather, don't pitch)

**YOU**
> "Cool. And just so I'm not assuming — how long have you been running Groupon for the studio?"

**THEM**
> "About a year and a half."

**YOU**
> "Got it. And out of curiosity — what made you start using them in the first place?"

**THEM**
> "Just wanted to get more people in the door."

**YOU**
> "Yeah, that makes sense. So when you started — what were you hoping it would do for the business?"

> ☝️ Now they're telling you their hopes. Their dream. They're emotionally engaged.

---

### STAGE 3 — PROBLEM AWARENESS (open the wound, don't sell)

**YOU**
> "And how's that gone? Like — is Groupon actually delivering the kind of customer you were hoping for?"

**THEM**
*(this is where the dam breaks)*
> "Honestly? Not really. They come once, use the deal, and we never see them again."

**YOU**
> "Hmm. So when you say 'never see them again' — like, what percentage do you think come back?"

**THEM**
> "Maybe 10%. Probably less."

**YOU**
> "Wow. Okay. And the cut they take — is that, like, 30%? 40%?"

**THEM**
> "Half. They take half."

**YOU**
*(long pause — let it sit)*
> "Hmm."

> ☝️ Do NOT speak for 2 seconds. The pause makes them keep talking. Miner calls this the "pregnant pause." Most reps blow this — they jump in to fix the prospect's pain. **Don't.** Let it hurt.

**THEM**
> "Yeah, it's not great honestly."

---

### STAGE 4 — CONSEQUENCE (make the pain stick)

**YOU**
> "Yeah, and I'm just curious — if you keep doing it this way for, like, the next 12 months… where do you think the studio ends up?"

**THEM**
> "I mean, it's tight. We need a better way to get new people in."

**YOU**
> "And when you say 'tight' — like, what does that look like day to day for you?"

**THEM**
> "Just stressful. I'm spending money on Groupon, Yelp, Instagram ads, and I can't tell what's working."

**YOU**
> "Got it. So if you took nothing else off your plate and just kept running things the way you are now — does that solve itself, or does it stay roughly the same?"

**THEM**
> "Stays the same. Or gets worse."

> ☝️ They just said it out loud. **You did not pitch.** They sold themselves on needing a fix.

---

### STAGE 5 — SOLUTION AWARENESS (let them imagine the fix)

**YOU**
> "Okay — so just hypothetically: if there was a way to get new customers in your door — but only people who actually live within 5 miles of you, who already have a reason to spend that day, like a birthday or anniversary — and there was no rev share, no contract, nothing taken off the top… would that be something worth a 90-second look?"

**THEM**
> "Yeah, that'd be huge."

> ☝️ "Worth a 90-second look" — soft commit. Not "buy now." You're earning the conversation.

**YOU**
> "Cool. Just so I make sure this is even a fit — when someone celebrates a birthday at your studio, do they typically come back? Like, do you see repeat traffic from celebrators?"

**THEM**
> "Oh yeah, for sure. Birthdays bring groups. They bring friends. Those people come back."

> ☝️ Now they just told you the ROI math. You didn't have to.

---

### STAGE 6 — QUALIFYING (frame the ask)

**YOU**
> "Got it. Last quick question before I tell you what we do — are you the person who'd decide whether to plug something like that into the studio, or is there a partner you'd want to loop in?"

**THEM**
> "It's just me."

**YOU**
> "Perfect. Okay — so the platform's called DASH. What we do is exactly that — we find people within a 5-mile radius of you who are celebrating a birthday or anniversary this week, and we connect them with an offer you post. You pay nothing for the first 90 days. After that it's $11.99 a month — flat, no contract, no rev share. Cancel anytime. And to be fair, the only people we want on the platform are people who actually want to be there, so if it doesn't work for you, just cancel."

*(pause — let them ask the next question)*

**THEM**
> "What's the catch?"

**YOU**
> "Honestly? The only catch is you have to post one offer. That's it. Takes 2 minutes. Most of our merchants do it from their phone while we're on the call."

---

### STAGE 7 — TRANSITION → COMMIT

**YOU**
> "So — based on what you just told me about Groupon, and what you said about birthdays bringing repeat traffic — does this sound like something worth trying for 90 days, where the worst case is you cancel and the best case is you stop relying on Groupon?"

**THEM**
> "Yeah, let's try it."

**YOU**
> "Cool. I'll walk you through it right now — takes 2 minutes. You near a phone or computer?"

## OBJECTION HANDLERS — NEPQ STYLE
*(Instead of defending, ask a question that makes them re-examine.)*

### OBJ 1 — "How are you different from Groupon?"
**v1 (Alan):** "Groupon takes a percentage of your fee, and their customers are discount seekers…"

**v2 (NEPQ):**
> "Yeah — totally fair question. Just curious, when you think of your Groupon customers vs. your best repeat customers, what's the actual difference between them?"
> *(pause — they describe it themselves)*
> "Exactly. So Groupon is built around the first kind. DASH is built around the second. People celebrating something within 5 miles — they come back because they live next door."

### OBJ 2 — "How do I know how many people came through?"
> "Great question. Would you rather check a dashboard, or get a text every Friday with the number? Most of our merchants tell us the text is way easier."
> *(They pick. Now they're configuring, not objecting.)*

### OBJ 3 — "Can I cancel anytime?"
> "Yeah, of course. Quick question — under what circumstances *would* you want to cancel? Like, what would have to be true for this not to be worth $11.99?"
> *(Now they're justifying *not* cancelling.)*

### OBJ 4 — "How do you get people to come in?"
> "Real quick — when someone celebrates their birthday near you, where do they usually find places to go? Like, what's the first thing they do?"
> *(They say "Google" or "Instagram." Now you say:)*
> "Right — so we're already there. We run paid placement across Meta, Google, and SMS push to celebrators inside your 5-mile radius. They see your offer right when they're deciding where to go."

### OBJ 5 — "I'm not interested."
> "Yeah, totally — and I respect that. Just out of curiosity, is it because you've tried platforms like this before and they didn't deliver, or is it more that the timing is bad right now?"
> *(One of those two is true. They'll tell you. Now you handle the real objection.)*

### OBJ 6 — "We're too busy right now."
> "Got it. So when you say busy — is that 'good busy, customers everywhere,' or is it 'I'm slammed but the studio's not where I want it'?"
> *(If the latter — they need DASH more, not less. Reframe.)*

### OBJ 7 — "Send me something in writing."
> "Sure thing. Before I send it, just so I send the right thing — are you more skeptical of the model itself, or more skeptical that it'll work in your specific neighborhood?"
> *(Now you can address the actual fear.)*

### OBJ 8 — "How much does it cost?"
> "It's $11.99 a month after 90 free days. But honestly, the better question is — how much is one birthday party of 6 people worth to your studio? Because that's what one celebrator brings, on average."

### OBJ 9 — "Does it actually work?"
> "Genuine question back — what would 'work' mean for you? Like, what number of new people walking in per month would make this a no-brainer?"
> *(They give you the number. You either match it or you walk away — both make you look credible.)*

### OBJ 10 — "I need to talk to my partner."
> "Totally — what's your partner most likely to want to know about it? Like, what would they ask if they were on the call right now?"
> *(Pre-handle their partner's objection. Suggest a 3-way call.)*

### OBJ 11 — "We tried something like this and it didn't work."
> "Oh, no kidding — what was the platform, and what specifically went sideways?"
> *(Listen. Distinguish DASH on the exact failure mode they describe.)*

### OBJ 12 — "Just email me."
> "Will do. Real quick before I do — if I sent you a one-pager and the math made sense, would you be the one to pull the trigger, or do you want me to loop someone else in too?"
> *(Qualifies the email follow-up. Don't waste sends on tire-kickers.)*

> "I run a yoga studio in Brooklyn."

> ☝️ This question is gold. Their answer tells you their pain. You then mirror it back through Stages 4–7 above.

## VOICEMAIL SCRIPT (when no answer)

> "Hey [Owner Name], this is [Your Name] from DASH. Quick reason for the call — I work with a bunch of studios in [Borough/Neighborhood] that are moving off Groupon. Wanted to see if it'd be worth a 5-minute conversation. My number is [###]. If now's bad, just shoot me a text and I'll work around your schedule. Thanks."

> ☝️ Why: name the neighborhood + name the competitor by name (Groupon) + low-friction callback. **Do not** explain DASH on a voicemail. The whole point is to make them call back curious.

## SCRIPT SUCCESS METRICS (TRACK THESE)

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Dial → live conversation | > 12% |
| Live conv → qualified | > 50% |
| Qualified → booked or set up | > 30% |
| Booked → activated (offer live) | > 80% |
| Activated → paying at Day 90 | > 60% |

**If conversation → qualified is below 50% — Stages 3 (Problem) and 4 (Consequence) aren't landing. Slow them down.**
**If qualified → booked is below 30% — Stage 6 (Qualifying) is being skipped. Reps are pitching too early.**
